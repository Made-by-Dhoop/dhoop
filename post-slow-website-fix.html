Why your site is slow

Every slow website is slow for a reason — it's never random, and it's never unfixable. The problem is that most business owners have no way of knowing which of the common causes applies to them, so they either ignore it or throw money at the wrong fix.

There are really only five causes worth checking, in roughly the order they're worth investigating. Most sites have at least two of these stacked on top of each other.

Cause 1: Page builders

If your site runs on Wix, Squarespace, Elementor, or a similar drag-and-drop tool, this is almost certainly your biggest issue. These platforms load a huge amount of JavaScript and CSS to support every possible feature — even the ones your page doesn't use.

You can optimise images and add caching on top of a page builder, and it'll help a little. But the underlying bloat stays. This is the one cause on this list that patching doesn't really fix — it needs a rebuild.

Cause 2: Unoptimised images

This is the easiest one to fix and one of the most common. A single unoptimised hero image can add 2-3 seconds to load time on its own — especially on mobile.

  • Use modern formats — WebP or AVIF instead of PNG or JPEG
  • Resize images to the dimensions they're actually displayed at, not the original camera resolution
  • Compress before uploading — tools like Squoosh or TinyPNG take seconds

Cause 3: Slow hosting

Cheap shared hosting means your site is queuing behind hundreds of other sites on the same server. This shows up as a slow Time to First Byte (TTFB) — the delay before your server even starts sending the page, before any of the actual content loads.

No amount of front-end optimisation fixes a slow server. If your TTFB is consistently above 600ms, hosting is worth investigating before anything else.

Cause 4: Third-party scripts

Chat widgets, tracking pixels, review plugins, font loaders — each one adds a request, and most of them block the page from rendering until they've loaded. It's common to find sites running 8-10 third-party scripts that nobody remembers adding.

A useful exercise: open your site in an incognito window and count how many separate services are loading in the background. Most business owners are surprised by the number.

Cause 5: No caching

Without proper cache headers, a returning visitor downloads every image, script, and stylesheet from scratch — every single time. Proper caching means their browser only re-downloads what's actually changed. This is usually a quick server-side fix, not a redesign.

Where to actually start

Don't guess. Run your site through pagespeed.web.dev and read the diagnostics section — it names the specific cause, not just the symptom. That tells you which of the five above actually applies to you.

If it comes back as a page builder problem, a rebuild is the only real fix. If it's images, hosting, or scripts, those can often be resolved without touching the rest of the site.

If you'd rather someone just tell you what's wrong and what it'll take to fix it, that's exactly what a health check is for.
